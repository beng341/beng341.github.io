**I am on the job market. I'm interested in academic jobs or research-based industry jobs in countries where I can work in English.**

I study the applications of social choice with a particular focus on the intersection of machine learning with social choice.

My broad interest is in collective decision-making: *What rules or mechanisms guide how groups (of humans or AIs) make joint actions?* The bite-sized chunk of this question where I focused much of my PhD research is on exploring the space of learnable voting rules -- learning new voting rules provides us decision-making tools well-adapted for any properties we consider important.

I have also worked in a range of applied topics relating to social choice, machine learning, and artificial intelligence:

- **Delegative Voting:** Liquid democracy has promising applications to ensemble systems and continual learning, in addition to being a cool tool for human settings!
- **Peer Review:** Classical social choice results guide the "micro review" process we use at the GAIW workshop. This forms the basis of a peer review format that might survive AI.
- **Recommender Systems:** Ideas of fairness and welfare from the economic and social choice literature capture ideas of fairness not well measured in current recommender systems.
- **Teaching AI Ethics:** I have quite enjoyed teaching about both ethical and policy issues of artificial intelligence at both the graduate and undergraduate level.
